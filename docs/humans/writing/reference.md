# Human-centered writing reference

This page offers optional prompts for human authors and editors. The normative requirements live in the canonical agent writing standard.

## A useful brief

Supply what matters for the task:

- deliverable and genre;
- reader and relationship;
- purpose or desired action;
- stakes and sensitivities;
- verified facts and material facts that must not change;
- position, tone, format, and length;
- representative voice samples, if appropriate;
- sources, required terms, and disclosure or compliance needs.

Not every field is required. A missing fact needs clarification only when it could materially change or mislead the result.

## Editorial lenses

Ask whether the draft begins with the real subject, gives the reader the needed context, selects rather than mechanically covers material, connects claims to evidence, matches the genre and relationship, preserves appropriate voice, and ends where the communication is complete.

Watch for clusters of situation-free generality, abstract importance, vague attribution, unsupported causality, assistant commentary, mechanical completeness, uniform paragraph roles, or generic closure. These are revision questions, not proof of AI authorship. Repair the missing fact, reasoning, selection, or genre fit rather than replacing isolated words.

Headings, bullets, passive voice, formal transitions, technical vocabulary, repeated defined terms, triads, contractions, and punctuation can all be correct. Judge their function in this document. Do not degrade accurate writing to satisfy a stereotype.

## Revision scope

- **Light edit:** correct errors and obvious friction while preserving content, organization, length, and voice.
- **Voice-preserving revision:** improve clarity and purpose without flattening characteristic directness, vocabulary, rhythm, or uncertainty.
- **Structural rewrite:** reorganize around the reader's task while preserving supported facts and position.
- **Audit:** identify supported reader-visible problems and corrections without automatically rewriting or overwriting the source.
- **Source-fidelity check:** compare the draft with supplied material.
- **Independent verification:** check an external claim, citation, command, or result against suitable evidence.

A review may legitimately find no issue. It should report every material issue it can support, not an arbitrary number.

## Illustrative revisions

These examples are fictional and show the kind of underlying repair to make, not phrases to copy or ban.

- Vague importance: “This change is important for reliability.” More useful: “Retrying only idempotent requests prevents a timeout from creating a duplicate order.”
- Unsupported certainty: “The migration will eliminate outages.” More honest: “If the staged rollback test passes, the migration removes the single-writer handoff that caused the two observed interruptions.”
- Incomplete procedure: “Run the setup command and verify it works.” More usable: “Run the supplied setup command, confirm it exits zero, then use the documented status command and compare the named healthy state.” Keep the real commands and expected output from verified project material rather than inventing them.

## Personal and application writing

For a résumé, cover letter, biography, or personal statement, use only supplied roles, dates, responsibilities, accomplishments, interests, and motivations. Quantify an outcome only when the source supports the number and the writer's contribution. Select evidence for the position or reader instead of listing everything.

A cover letter may connect supported experience to the stated role, but it must not invent familiarity with an organization, enthusiasm, relationships, or commitments. A personal statement may be reflective when the author supplied that position or voice evidence; do not manufacture adversity, vulnerability, mistakes, emotion, or a life lesson to create a more “human” narrative.

## Optional author review

A human author may use two passes when the stakes justify them:

1. Check meaning and integrity: material facts, support, qualifications, protected content, disclosures, and the requested outcome.
2. Check the reader experience: selection, order, clarity, genre, relationship, voice, and the natural ending.

This is an editing aid, not proof that external claims were independently verified, and it is not a required sequence for every document.

## Integrity reminders

Do not invent anecdotes, experience, dialogue, emotion, metrics, citations, credentials, mistakes, slang, or vulnerability to simulate humanity. Label fictional examples. Keep required AI-use disclosures. If a command, citation, result, or empirical claim has not been independently checked, say so when that gap matters.
