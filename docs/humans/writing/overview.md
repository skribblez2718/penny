# Human-centered writing in Penny

Penny's writing policy aims to make reader-facing material useful for its actual situation. It does not try to hide AI assistance or mimic a universal human style.

A short principle in the Cognitive Frame applies to everyday prose. Substantive drafting, revision, and editorial review use the canonical agent guidance under `docs/agents/writing/`: one shared standard, a review guide, and small genre-specific additions. Closed workflow workers and private knowledge-base phases receive applicable canonical documents from the host; they do not depend on ambient file discovery.

The standard prioritizes truth, source meaning, required disclosures, technical precision, the active output contract, and the reader's task. Voice samples affect style, not facts, identity, permissions, feelings, or commitments. Surface patterns such as headings, formal language, passive voice, punctuation, or repeated terms are contextual choices rather than banned features.

Optional prompt templates provide convenient starting points for drafting, revision, and audit. They do not overwrite a source, publish material, or authorize external action by themselves.

See [the advisory reference](reference.md) for briefing questions, editorial cues, and examples. The reference is not a second normative standard.
