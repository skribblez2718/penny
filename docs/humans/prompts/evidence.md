# Prompt Architecture Evidence Base

What the 2024–2026 empirical literature actually supports, what it debunks, and which of Penny's design principles are evidence-backed versus house hypotheses. Compiled 2026-07 from the sources in [References](#references).

## Evidence tiers used across the prompt docs

| Tag                     | Meaning                                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **[EVIDENCE]**          | Replicated, cross-model published results support it                                    |
| **[HYPOTHESIS]**        | Plausible and internally consistent, but not established in the literature              |
| **[DEBUNKED-ADJACENT]** | The nearby popular claim failed replication; our usage survives only in a narrower form |

## Per-technique verdicts (the short version)

| Technique                                                            | Verdict                            | What the numbers say                                                                                                                                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Chain-of-thought prompting                                           | Robust but narrow                  | +12–14 pts on math/symbolic, **+0.7 pts on everything else** (meta-analysis of 1,218 comparisons); near-zero to negative on reasoning-native models, which all vendors say not to CoT-prompt |
| Self-consistency (vote over samples)                                 | Robust, diminishing                | +4–18 pts at 10–40× cost on older models; gains shrink as base accuracy rises                                                                                                                |
| Clear, complete, specific instructions                               | Robust                             | The one intervention that helps everywhere; wording/format idiosyncrasies swamp technique choice in the Prompt Report's own case studies                                                     |
| Position (front-load / end-load critical content)                    | Robust                             | U-shaped attention ("lost in the middle") replicates broadly                                                                                                                                 |
| Light structural delimiting (tags/headers when mixing content types) | Robust as hygiene                  | Reduces misinterpretation; **no universally best format exists** — best format is model-specific                                                                                             |
| Structured output (JSON mode)                                        | Mixed                              | Neutral to +4% with matched prompts and reasoning-before-answer field order; naive answer-only schemas genuinely hurt weak models                                                            |
| Persona/role prompting for accuracy                                  | Debunked                           | 162 personas × 4 model families: no gain, per-persona effects "largely random"; 9 significant _decreases_ across 6 frontier models                                                           |
| Emotional appeals, politeness, tips/threats                          | Debunked                           | Null aggregate effects with huge per-question variance — a lottery ticket, not a lever                                                                                                       |
| Intrinsic self-correction ("review your answer")                     | Debunked without external feedback | GPT-4 _lost_ 4 pts on GSM8K after self-review; no prior work shows successful intrinsic self-correction                                                                                      |
| Prompt transfer across models                                        | Strongly negative                  | Best-format overlap across families IoU < 0.2; optimized prompts lose ~6–11 pts absolute when ported; format choice alone swings results up to 76 pts                                        |
| Automatic per-model prompt optimization (DSPy/MIPRO/GEPA-style)      | Robust when an eval metric exists  | Reliably beats expert hand-tuning; gains are model-specific by construction                                                                                                                  |

## What this means for Penny's goal

The strong form of "one universal prompt that adds points on any model" is **not supported** — prompts are model-specific artifacts. The defensible reframe, which the architecture now adopts ([Overview](overview.md)):

1. **The universal layer raises the floor and cuts variance.** Completeness, explicitness, non-contradiction, position, and structural hygiene are the cross-model survivors. They mostly prevent losses; they are not where double-digit gains live.
2. **Task-specification quality is the best-evidenced universal lever** — the Invocation Context should state the goal, constraints, success criteria, and material unknowns clearly.

## Verbal uncertainty calibration

Confidence words are commonly miscalibrated. Groot & Valdenegro-Toro (2024) evaluated verbalized uncertainty across models and found substantial overconfidence: a model saying "probably" or "certainly" does not establish that the proposition succeeds at any known rate. **Implication for Penny:** evidence status (source-backed, tool-verified, inferred, assumed, unknown) is the safer user-facing vocabulary, because it explains _why_ a claim should be trusted rather than attaching a confidence adjective. The `CERTAIN / PROBABLE / POSSIBLE / UNCERTAIN` scale in agent output contracts is a **controlled vocabulary the engine parses**, not a calibrated probability — do not describe it as "calibrated" unless calibration is actually measured against outcomes.

## Prompt security: delimiters are hygiene, not enforcement

Structural delimiters (XML tags, headers) measurably reduce content-type confusion — that is the hygiene finding above. There is no evidence they create an enforceable privilege boundary: instruction-hierarchy adherence is imperfect in all evaluated models (IHEval), and hierarchy improvements come from _training_ models to prioritize privileged messages (Wallace et al. 2024), not from forceful adjectives in the prompt. Pi itself has no built-in sandbox. **Implication for Penny:** markers are defense-in-depth cues; enforceable limits live in the runtime control plane (tool allowlists, approvals/receipts, path-specific process isolation, OS permissions). See [Security Architecture](security-architecture.md).

## Independent evidence vs. model-diverse review

Intrinsic self-correction fails without new external information (Huang et al. 2024; Kamoi et al. 2024). A _different_ model reviewing the same output helps with overlooked issues and assumption-challenging, but it is not independent grounding: models share training data, priors, blind spots, and often the same incomplete context, so correlated errors survive the review. **Ranking for consequential verification:** tests, compilers, static analysis, current authoritative sources, observed system state, and separately collected data are independent evidence; a second model is supplementary model-diverse review. Penny's docs and prompts must not describe a different model as "independent verification."

## Lean prompts

Directional evidence favors leaner permanent context. Anthropic's context-engineering guidance recommends the smallest set of high-signal tokens; OpenAI reported that in internal coding-agent evaluations, leaner prompt configurations improved scores ~10–15% while cutting tokens 41–66% and cost 33–67%. These are workload-specific results, not a forecast for Penny — the honest posture is: prefer one authoritative statement of each invariant over cross-layer repetition, and **validate against Penny's own evals** (`make evals`) rather than importing another lab's percentages.
