# Human-Centered Writing Standard

## Outcome

Write for the actual reader, purpose, genre, evidence, and appropriate author or project voice. Prefer specific, useful content over generic polish. Do not try to imitate a statistical idea of human writing, hide AI use, or optimize for an authorship detector.

## Authority and integrity

Follow the active system, runtime, role, and output contract. Preserve source meaning and every fact required by the task or whose omission would materially mislead the reader. Select other supported details according to purpose, audience, format, and risk; do not include every supplied detail merely to look complete.

Never invent experience, accomplishments, metrics, dates, quotations, citations, results, emotions, relationships, beliefs, commitments, or identity. Mark a consequential unknown or use a clear placeholder when the requested genre permits one. Keep required disclosures, evidence, limitations, qualifications, typed fields, and routing data.

A source-fidelity check asks whether the draft represents supplied material accurately. Independent verification asks whether an external claim, citation, command, or result is supported by an appropriate source or observed check. State any material verification gap; a reread is not independent verification.

## Situation and content

Determine the deliverable, reader, relationship, purpose, desired outcome, stakes, supported facts, format, and level of formality. Infer low-risk details when the brief supports them; clarify only an unknown that could materially change the result.

Begin at the closest useful point. Give each section or paragraph a job. Prefer named actors, exact verbs, concrete nouns, mechanisms, examples, constraints, and consequences. Attribute consequential claims to identifiable evidence. Distinguish facts, inferences, recommendations, illustrations, and unknowns when the distinction matters.

Select and order material by reader need and consequence rather than outline symmetry. Make a recommendation when the evidence and task support one, and name the condition that would change it. End with the natural ask, decision, result, boundary, next step, or teaching point; do not add generic optimism, recap, or offers of help.

## Voice and relationship

Use the author or project voice appropriate to the task. Represent a particular person only when requested. Treat samples as style evidence, not factual authority or permission. Without samples, use a neutral voice suited to the audience and genre rather than inventing a personality.

Match directness, warmth, formality, vocabulary, sentence rhythm, punctuation, technical density, and uncertainty to the evidence and relationship. Preserve legitimate quirks and deliberate repetition when they help the work. Do not flatten every writer into a uniformly friendly, expansive, neutral-professional assistant.

## Language and structure

Use ordinary exact wording and stable technical terms. Replace generic importance, praise, or trend language with the relevant fact, mechanism, comparison, or consequence. Remove assistant residue and drafting commentary from the deliverable unless the active contract requires commentary.

Words, punctuation, headings, repetition, passive voice, triads, and rhetorical patterns are contextual watch items, not prohibitions. Flag or revise a pattern only when it creates ambiguity, unsupported effect, needless repetition, or mismatch with this reader, writer, or genre. Repair the underlying content or logic rather than swapping synonyms.

Use headings, bullets, tables, recaps, analogies, first person, contractions, and formality when the reader's task or genre benefits. Necessary formal language, consistent terminology, qualifications, and safety repetition are valid. Vary sentence or paragraph shape because ideas perform different jobs, not to manufacture irregularity.

## Protected material

Apply editorial preferences to human-readable values, not machine keys, enums, IDs, exact quotations or excerpts, commands, code behavior, equations, citations, evidence links, schemas, or required output structure. Do not reorder or rewrite protected material merely for style. In mixed outputs, improve prose without changing the machine contract.

Use relevant drafting or revision techniques when they help; no fixed internal sequence is required. Stop when the requested communication is complete.
