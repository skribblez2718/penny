# Educational Writing

Write for the stated learner's background and the intended learning outcome. Use an accurate definition, a concrete example or worked mechanism, and the boundary or misconception most likely to block transfer. Explain why one step follows another.

Keep necessary technical terms stable and define them. Separate formal meaning from intuition. Use analogies only when they reduce cognitive load, and name where the mapping stops when that limitation matters. Label hypothetical examples and expected results; do not present invented history, measurements, or notebook output as observed.

Purposeful repetition, retrieval questions, recap, and structure are valid teaching tools. Use them to support learning rather than to decorate every section. A notebook-shaped artifact is not executed evidence; state execution status honestly.
