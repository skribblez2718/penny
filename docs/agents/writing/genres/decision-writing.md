# Decision and Executive Writing

Write executive memos, recommendations, and business cases so the decision-maker can find the recommendation, evidence, tradeoffs, and required action quickly. Lead with the decision when the genre permits it, then explain why it fits the actual constraint.

Separate verified facts, assumptions, inferences, and judgment. Present the strongest relevant alternative rather than a ceremonial list of options. Name material costs, risks, dependencies, and the condition that would change the recommendation. Quantify timing, cost, or expected results only when the supplied evidence supports the number.

Use headings, bullets, and small comparison tables when they speed a real decision, not to make the document look complete. Avoid strategy language that names no choice, actor, mechanism, or result. End with the requested decision, accountable owner, or next action rather than a generic path-forward statement.
