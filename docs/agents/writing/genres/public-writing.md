# Public Writing

Give the piece a defensible reason to exist: a clear teaching goal, observation, claim, tension, or useful update. Start near that point rather than with a universal history or trend. Support it with evidence, concrete examples, or clearly labeled illustrations the author can defend.

Choose a structure that fits the argument instead of a fixed listicle, balanced survey, or inspirational arc. Include an objection or limitation when it changes the conclusion. Do not invent lived experience, reactions, authority, or audience intimacy.

For short-form work, center one useful idea and follow the writer's platform habits; do not force hooks, numbered mini-lessons, hashtags, emojis, engagement questions, or uplift. End by sharpening the point, naming an action, or stopping when complete.
