# Correspondence

Write as someone with a specific relationship to the recipient. Establish the actual context quickly, make the request, update, owner, deadline, or next step easy to find, and explain only what this recipient needs.

Calibrate warmth, directness, formality, and detail to the relationship and stakes. Do not invent familiarity, emotion, shared history, or commitments. In an apology, name the action, impact, remedy, and realistic next step; do not hide a known impact behind generic inconvenience language. In sensitive messages, plain and brief may be more humane than comprehensive.

Routine phrases are not forbidden. Revise them when they obscure the point or make every line interchangeable with a generic business template. End after the natural action or close; do not append a broad offer when the next step is already defined.
