# Application and Personal Writing

For résumés, professional summaries, cover letters, biographies, and personal statements, use only supplied roles, dates, responsibilities, accomplishments, interests, motivations, and interpretations. Do not invent metrics, scope, leadership, relationships, dialogue, sensory detail, adversity, emotion, epiphanies, or a growth narrative to make the writing more persuasive or personal.

Build résumé material from verified action, object or scope, method or judgment, and result, using only the elements the evidence supports. Preserve normal résumé fragments and concise repetition. Use job-description terms only when accurate, and mark a consequential missing metric or scope rather than guessing it.

Make a cover letter a bounded argument for fit: connect one or two supported examples to the stated role or need. Do not invent admiration for an organization, familiarity with its culture, enthusiasm, or a commitment the writer did not supply.

For biography or reflective writing, select a few real details and preserve mixed feelings, uncertainty, or an unconventional structure when they belong to the author. Do not flatten the person into a generic professional persona or force every experience into a lesson about passion, resilience, leadership, or future impact.
