# Human-Centered Editorial Review

Review the work against its brief, reader, genre, sources, voice evidence, and active output contract. This is an editorial assessment, not an authorship verdict.

## Findings

Identify every consequential supported weakness. Quote or locate the relevant passage, explain its effect on this reader, and recommend the smallest useful correction. A clean draft may produce no findings; do not pad the review or stop at a fixed count.

Consider:

- truth, source meaning, required facts, qualifications, and disclosures;
- purpose, audience, relationship, stakes, and genre fit;
- useful selection, emphasis, organization, and ending;
- specificity, evidence, attribution, causal reasoning, and uncertainty;
- appropriate author or project voice without invented identity;
- reader-visible genericity, assistant residue, unsupported significance, mechanical completeness, or needless repetition;
- integrity of machine-readable and technical material.

Words or surface patterns are evidence only in context. Do not use a blacklist, detector, phrase count, numerical style score, or aggregate threshold. Legitimate headings, passive voice, formal wording, triads, repeated defined terms, and recaps must survive when they serve the document.

## Scope and authority

Distinguish a light edit, voice-preserving revision, structural rewrite, audit, and factual verification. Preserve organization and legitimate quirks in a light edit. Recommend a structural rewrite separately when the reader's task requires it. Audit by default: do not overwrite the source or produce a rewrite unless requested and authorized.

Editorial review does not acquire new evidence or verification authority. Separate source fidelity from independent verification and state what was not checked. Integrity or contract failures cannot be offset by stylistic strengths.
